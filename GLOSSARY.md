<a id="level-one-cheese"></a>

## Level one cheese

An opening ambush designed to win the first experience exchange. In Ramy’s Teemo version, he gets behind the opposing laner, prepares his passive invisibility, then uses the attack-speed bonus when he reveals himself to threaten a fight. **The main payoff is denying the opponent XP while collecting his own.** A kill is a possible outcome; chasing one can defeat the purpose if it costs the wave.

His dedicated guide explains the intended sequence: secure the approach, hide near the opponent’s route to the first wave, wait for the enemy wave’s last caster to start attacking, then threaten the opponent away from XP range. He hides again as the next wave arrives to avoid drawing its attention too early. The exact hiding spots are visual demonstrations in the video, not positions that can be reliably reconstructed from phrases such as “here” in the transcript.

- **Goal and passive use:** [Dedicated cheese guide, 01:00](transcripts/gyWGLQYigsE.md#p-1c5f79ae6d46f78477c75132156d66f1c67ecdad8e094d397b443d6982851441) · Uploaded 2026-02-06 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gyWGLQYigsE&t=60s
- **When to reveal and why XP matters:** [First-wave execution, 05:00](transcripts/gyWGLQYigsE.md#p-4041517a2604a1753f9bde071e5fcb8f93658fcebb6be905a80810ea0be89f21) · Uploaded 2026-02-06 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gyWGLQYigsE&t=300s
- **Repeat the pressure without catching the arriving wave:** [Second-wave execution, 05:30](transcripts/gyWGLQYigsE.md#p-9f60b3503da6c534c050dadf2ff62da0e6b5f6f97d4efde922bf87713b009a42) · Uploaded 2026-02-06 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gyWGLQYigsE&t=330s
- **A coached example:** [Zoning Kennen from the first minions, 02:30](transcripts/02P9C6UHfjI.md#p-07b0d49ee2868b4f4a80de4c91a3a6c3ca64889863fbee89f74cae06a7893858) · Uploaded 2026-09-08 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=02P9C6UHfjI&t=150s

<a id="passive-and-invis"></a>

## Passive / invis

In these Teemo passages, preparing or getting his passive means setting up the champion’s invisibility and the attack-speed advantage when he comes out of it. “Invis” is shorthand for invisible. Hiding can support an ambush, a renewed trade or a way to wait out pressure; it is not a claim that Teemo is safe from every enemy ability.

Ramy explicitly prepares his passive for extra attack speed before a possible trade against Darius. [Preparing the next trade, 00:30](transcripts/YAZEYclWYqc.md#p-8313fc603947f5e13201373f2430a1723592b53226c943f0befda3a7d16d3219) · Uploaded 2026-08-22 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YAZEYclWYqc&t=30s. The [level one cheese](#level-one-cheese) uses the same idea for early XP pressure. Exact activation timings and reveal interactions require checking the applicable patch.

<a id="zoning-and-xp-denial"></a>

## Zoning / XP denial

**Zoning** means using the threat of a bad trade or death to keep an opponent away from an area. In lane, denying a last hit costs the opponent gold; pushing them far enough away to miss experience can also delay their levels. Ramy’s cheese prioritises that second result, while keeping Teemo close enough to receive the XP himself.

His coaching example explicitly prioritises denying the first melee minions’ XP over taking every last hit. [Denying the next wave, 03:00](transcripts/02P9C6UHfjI.md#p-db798589c21c6dfe9892e77dbc84ff67b4b3ec6b7cd0f0c5dbc9478cec789ea0) · Uploaded 2026-09-08 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=02P9C6UHfjI&t=180s. In another game he distinguishes missed gold from missed XP. [What the opponent actually loses, 09:58](transcripts/_aAZMW255RU.md#p-26e6cafcf3451e53c63458eb9ceb23a0e1393211b10d56076bb9b0a8d577c487) · Uploaded 2026-09-14 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_aAZMW255RU&t=599s.

<a id="slow-push"></a>

## Slow push / stacking a wave

A slow push lets an allied wave build up while it advances, instead of clearing the enemy minions as quickly as possible. In Ramy’s examples, delaying his last hits extends the time an opponent is zoned away and helps accumulate a larger wave. The resulting wave can support a crash, turret pressure or a reset; it does not automatically make a dive safe.

He explains why he wants a larger wave, but also why Kennen’s health, stun and his own missing Ignite make the dive risky. [Building the wave without forcing a dive, 08:30](transcripts/Z86RdWK4P3s.md#p-e316be42bd9ad92fb75a0dbe809040fd8fd88107f4068ee1e1fa06166aa6d9b4) · Uploaded 2026-09-12 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z86RdWK4P3s&t=510s. He later distinguishes a slow push from a freeze. [Last-hitting as late as possible, 10:30](transcripts/Z86RdWK4P3s.md#p-84736d1919ba9862d8aae0999e097b5ef0f63a5f7b9bdc861da4fda8c91229e4) · Uploaded 2026-09-12 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z86RdWK4P3s&t=631s.

<a id="crash"></a>

## Crash / shove / fast push

**Shoving** or **fast pushing** means clearing the enemy wave quickly. A **crash** is the result when your surviving minions reach the enemy turret, making the opponent deal with them there. Starting to push does not guarantee a completed crash: an opponent may hold the wave outside turret range.

Ramy changes from a slow push to a fast push once the wave is going under the turret anyway, intending to use the time for turret damage or harassment. [Finishing the push, 11:30](transcripts/Z86RdWK4P3s.md#p-966f7f1756b432d5afb799affc8be41633d13d56b647b4eb1fb62f211eedc3f0) · Uploaded 2026-09-12 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z86RdWK4P3s&t=691s. A completed crash is also the starting point for a [cheater recall](#cheater-recall).

<a id="bounce"></a>

## Bounce / return wave

A bounce is the wave’s direction reversing after a crash, so the lane starts pushing back towards the player who previously pushed it out. Ramy often wants this because it brings farm towards his side and makes the opposing laner step farther away from their turret to contest it.

He describes planning a crash and bounce against Garen. [Planning the lane before it starts, 04:00](transcripts/Z86RdWK4P3s.md#p-6f9966586202015af57d40fa436f996246dc5a5148fe2e1feed08464e431bed1) · Uploaded 2026-09-12 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z86RdWK4P3s&t=240s. Against Kennen, he explains letting the wave come towards him to force the opponent to walk up. [Using the incoming wave, 09:00](transcripts/Z86RdWK4P3s.md#p-359676d8b4ce221affbdd277bb1cf366b8c4f527107caf05abe3f90e2498d764) · Uploaded 2026-09-12 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z86RdWK4P3s&t=540s. The actual minion state matters; this is not a promise that every shove produces the desired bounce.

<a id="cheater-recall"></a>

## Cheater recall

A **cheater recall** is a planned lane reset that uses a wave crash to buy time: build a slow push, push the stacked wave fully into the enemy turret, then recall, shop and heal while the opponent clears it. The aim is to walk back as the wave pushes towards you, losing little or no farm or experience and avoiding the need to spend Teleport. You return with resources and an item advantage while the opponent still needs a reset. This is an established wave-management term, not cheating or an exploit. Terminology reference: [PHuffZone's explanation, published 27 August 2020](https://mobalytics.gg/blog/lol-top-lane-wave-management-guide-cheater-recall/) — https://mobalytics.gg/blog/lol-top-lane-wave-management-guide-cheater-recall/ . Its old timings and shopping lists are not current-patch recommendations.

## Freeze / holding the wave

A freeze keeps the minion fight near a chosen position, commonly outside your own turret, by controlling how quickly minions die rather than clearing everything. It can force an opponent to approach an unsafe part of the lane or lose farm. Simply stopping attacks does not guarantee a lasting freeze: the surviving minions and incoming waves must sustain it.

Against Darius, Ramy says pushing was a mistake because the frozen wave had denied Darius a useful lane option; pushing gave him a way back into the game. [Breaking a useful freeze, 08:00](transcripts/YAZEYclWYqc.md#p-e45dd12f5158cd3ab23f5469fc6a6d377e3f505348f5712de38a80a0a2d7bab8) · Uploaded 2026-08-22 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YAZEYclWYqc&t=480s. In the Kennen game he explicitly says the minion balance will produce a slow push rather than a freeze. [Recognising the difference, 10:30](transcripts/Z86RdWK4P3s.md#p-84736d1919ba9862d8aae0999e097b5ef0f63a5f7b9bdc861da4fda8c91229e4) · Uploaded 2026-09-12 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z86RdWK4P3s&t=631s.

<a id="spacing"></a>

## Spacing

Spacing is managing the distance between champions so you can threaten your own attacks while limiting the opponent’s response. For Teemo, Ramy often describes stepping around an opponent’s ability range, using bushes, and choosing when a return hit is worth taking. It is a decision about distance and timing, not simply running away.

Against Gangplank, he discusses avoiding Q poke through spacing and bush use, while judging one exchange worthwhile for a Press the Attack proc. [Spacing a poke ability, 01:00](transcripts/Pbs7kCX6-Xw.md#p-a7ae434f913d2d72392952f43f7b523902e5e461f6c20506c3b1da5bf3257f16) · Uploaded 2026-05-20 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pbs7kCX6-Xw&t=60s. That recorded trade is an example of the judgement, not a current-patch damage calculation.

<a id="trade-and-all-in"></a>

## Trade / poke / all-in

**Poke** is damage intended to wear the opponent down. A **trade** is an exchange after which you expect to disengage. An **all-in** is committing to an extended fight for a kill, potentially spending summoner spells. Ramy distinguishes having enough tools for a short exchange from having enough to finish the opponent.

In the Jax game, he says having Ignite would have changed his decision to all-in. [A missing summoner changes the decision, 27:01](transcripts/zVoOvrr0bh8.md#p-85a0abc29c9acbc442589bbc465f2d4f8cec2b252a639bb09697233441a455bc) · Uploaded 2026-09-17 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVoOvrr0bh8&t=1622s. In another matchup, he warns against continuing after Q because the opponent can Cleanse and commit onto him. [Ending the trade, 01:00](transcripts/SULBrVIR3XQ.md#p-89410abd9d93225dc9f8754f9438e2b685e0e886ab654e107b115f4d700ef936) · Uploaded 2026-06-09 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SULBrVIR3XQ&t=60s.

<a id="priority"></a>

## Priority / prio

Lane priority is the practical ability to act or move before the opposing laner, often because your wave is pushed and they must answer it. Ramy uses “prio” when discussing whether a team can support a jungle play, enter an objective area or move off the lane. It is temporary and depends on who can actually move, not just who has more kills.

He criticises an attempt at Grubs when neither top nor mid can help: one laner is in base and he needs to recall. [An objective attempt without lane support, 08:30](transcripts/j9HVq5mQE_Y.md#p-d65575956b66ca391979b406c94a1741cb48a130033ab2a388a6671494829ce5) · Uploaded 2026-08-07 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j9HVq5mQE_Y&t=510s. Before Baron, he connects mid priority with getting control of the area to place shrooms. [Priority before setup, 28:59](transcripts/9j8ZvwND_uQ.md#p-cdf940aa1e86120d9871e93c1d0be5501b1e7b77a90c24ba484fd55450172459) · Uploaded 2026-08-09 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9j8ZvwND_uQ&t=1740s.

## Reset / recall timer / tempo

In this lane-and-map context, a **reset** means recalling to restore resources and spend gold. A **recall timer** is a window to do that at an acceptable cost in missed minions or map pressure. **Tempo** is being ready to make the next useful move sooner: an extra camp or wave can be less valuable than returning in time for a fight. Not every reset is a [cheater recall](#cheater-recall).

Ramy declines extra Krugs because drake is approaching, choosing to recall and return to the map on time. [Protecting the next objective timing, 15:30](transcripts/g2SSwQrSJgM.md#p-d24421343fb208e8ae067f35f4c9ffe012d63762e0aa8bbfb424cecb694ddf1e) · Uploaded 2026-08-20 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g2SSwQrSJgM&t=930s. Elsewhere he times his recall to leave base alongside Tristana. [Matching a teammate’s timing, 21:30](transcripts/TXoUtYWYOf8.md#p-f85ab67a302ee5afc32c10887efeea5dea5e382fe45341698c105ef1c3b43b12) · Uploaded 2026-08-24 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TXoUtYWYOf8&t=1290s. In other contexts, “reset” can refer to a kill-triggered ability refresh or an objective returning to its starting state; read the surrounding sentence.

<a id="weakside"></a>

## Weakside / strongside

Weakside is the part of the map receiving less allied support while the team puts resources or attention elsewhere. It does not necessarily mean the lane matchup is losing in isolation. Ramy uses the term when enemy jungle pressure limits his opportunities while his own team is playing towards the other side.

He describes being unable to take a lane opportunity because the enemy jungler is playing top, and contrasts his weakside position with his bot lane’s strongside position. [Uneven jungle support, 03:00](transcripts/FFjfusfvyqs.md#p-d2ee79653d455c1f5604dc0a87bb4d54fec4b2ea3ae7310276f7e756c07b674b) · Uploaded 2026-03-29 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FFjfusfvyqs&t=180s. The implication is to judge what can be taken safely with the support available, not to assume a duel stays a duel.

<a id="shroom-setup"></a>

## Shroom setup / fighting on your terms

Shrooms are Teemo’s placed mushrooms. **Setup** means placing them and establishing access to an area before a likely fight, so opponents have to enter ground that is already prepared. Ramy connects this with wave priority, vision and time before an objective; having mushroom charges alone does not mean he can safely walk in to use them.

In the educational Kennen game, he wants to draw the opponents towards his Baron setup rather than contest drake without preparation. [Choosing the prepared area, 28:01](transcripts/Z86RdWK4P3s.md#p-f84c547eaf7991ae640359eb0c74f45c7034ca914030208637f2c93a33dc2839) · Uploaded 2026-09-12 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z86RdWK4P3s&t=1682s. He then notes that the fight still goes badly because his team needed to turn onto the opponents. [Setup is an advantage, not a guaranteed win, 29:01](transcripts/Z86RdWK4P3s.md#p-c94540182d7032743bd27c4e945bc23728dfcf2431cd3daa218baa4ddc4bbd7c) · Uploaded 2026-09-12 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z86RdWK4P3s&t=1742s.

<a id="dusk-and-dawn-trick"></a>

## Dusk and Dawn trick

This is Ramy’s name for an item interaction he describes while sustaining in lane: begin Q on a minion that is about to die to an auto attack, with the intended result that Q does not complete but Dusk and Dawn still triggers and provides healing. This entry records his explanation; it does not establish that the interaction works on the current patch.

He explains the sequence directly during the educational Kennen game. [His explanation of the trick, 17:00](transcripts/Z86RdWK4P3s.md#p-6f1dedb04593c6d6b43353cf926ba2811d7aefa9c2bb73b61566ae8cfc5e11ff) · Uploaded 2026-09-12 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z86RdWK4P3s&t=1021s. **Especially patch-sensitive:** item triggers and cancelled casts can change or be fixed. Treat this as a term to recognise in the recording, not a verified present-day mechanic.
